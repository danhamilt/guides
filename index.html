Your First AI Helper

A gentle, picture-by-picture guide to setting up an AI assistant on your own computer — one that can draw diagrams and build little web pages for you. No experience needed. You cannot break anything.

About 30 minutes Windows computer Costs about $5 to start Written for beginners
0 of 5 steps done

1. What you are making

Imagine a helper that sits inside a program on your computer. You type what you want in ordinary English — "draw me a diagram of my garden" or "make me a birthday invitation as a web page" — and it makes the file. Then, with one drag, you can put that file on the internet and send someone the link.

That is the whole idea. Here it is as a picture:

The whole journey, start to finish.

Five words you will meet

EditorThe program on your computer where the work happens. We will use a free one called VS Code.
HelperThe AI assistant that sits inside the editor and does the typing. We will use one called Cline.
KeyA long secret password that lets the helper think. It looks like sk-.... You will get one from DeepSeek.
DeepSeekThe company that provides the "thinking". You pay them a few dollars as you go.
FolderA place on your computer where your projects live. The helper can only see the folder you open.
The most important thing on this page

Nothing here can damage your computer. If something looks wrong, you can close the program and start again. There is no mistake you can make that cannot be undone.

2. Before you start

Have these ready. That is all you need.

  • A Windows computer (this works on Mac too — the buttons just look slightly different).
  • An internet connection.
  • A payment card, for about $5 of credit. Think of it like a coffee. It lasts a long time.
  • A phone or password notebook, to keep your secret key safe.
  • Half an hour, unhurried.
A note on the $5

The helper charges you a tiny amount each time it thinks — usually a fraction of a cent. Five dollars is plenty to play with for a good while. You will set this up in Step 3.

3. The five steps

Tick each box as you finish it. Your ticks are saved, so you can close this page and come back.

Step 1. Get the editor (VS Code)

This is the free program where everything happens.

  1. Open your web browser and go to code.visualstudio.com.
  2. Click the big blue button that says Download for Windows.
  3. When the download finishes, click the file it saved (it will be in your Downloads folder, named something like VSCodeUserSetup).
  4. Click Next through the installer and then Install. You do not need to change any of the options.
  5. When it finishes, VS Code will open. It may look plain and a little technical. That is normal.

Diagram 1 — The VS Code download page. Click the blue button.

What success looks like

You have a program called Visual Studio Code you can open from your Start menu. Its icon is a small blue ribbon.

Step 2. Add the AI helper (Cline)

Cline is a free add-on for VS Code. It is the friendly face you will actually talk to.

  1. With VS Code open, look at the tall strip of icons down the far left. Click the one that looks like four small squares (it is called Extensions, and there is a badge on it).
  2. A panel opens. Click the search box at the top and type Cline.
  3. The first result should be called Cline. Click its Install button.
  4. Wait a moment. When it is installed, a small robot face appears in that left-hand strip. Click it.

Diagram 2 — Finding and installing Cline.

What success looks like

A small robot face appears in the left strip, and a chat panel opens on the right. It will ask you to set up a "provider" — that is Step 4. Do not worry about it yet.

Step 3. Get your secret key from DeepSeek

This is the part that sounds technical but is really just filling in a form and copying a password. Take it slowly.

  1. In your browser, go to platform.deepseek.com and click Sign up. Use your email address. Check your email for a code and type it in.
  2. Find Billing in the menu on the left, and add about $5 of credit. (The page may show prices in a different currency, like ¥. That is normal — pick the smallest top-up.)
  3. Now click API Keys in the same left-hand menu, then click Create new API key.
  4. Give it a name you will recognise later, like my-laptop. Click create.
  5. A long code starting with sk- appears. Copy it right now — it is shown only once. Paste it into your phone's notes or a password notebook and save it.

Diagram 3 — Top up, then create and copy your key.

Treat the key like a card number

Never put it in an email, a text message, a photo, or a shared document. Anyone who has it can spend your credit. Keep it in your password notebook, and in the box in the next step. Nowhere else.

Step 4. Introduce the helper to your key

Now we tell Cline where to think. This is copy-and-paste — no typing of long codes.

  1. Back in VS Code, click the little robot face in the left strip to open Cline.
  2. Click the small gear (settings) icon at the top of the Cline panel.
  3. Find the box called API Provider and choose DeepSeek from the list.
  4. Paste your key into the API Key box.
  5. For the model, choose deepseek-flash. If you do not see it in the list, type it in by hand exactly like that, all lowercase.
  6. Click Done or Save.

Diagram 4 — Three boxes and a button. That is the whole setup.

If the model list shows old names

If you see deepseek-chat or deepseek-reasoner in the list, ignore them — they are retired and no longer work. Type deepseek-flash in by hand instead.

Step 5. Ask it for something

The moment you have been waiting for. First, make a home for your projects:

  1. On your Desktop, right-click → New → Folder. Name it My Projects.
  2. In VS Code, click File → Open Folder, and choose that folder.
  3. Click the robot face to open Cline, type this, and press Enter:
Hello! Please make me a simple one-page website that says
"Hello, my name is [your name]." Keep it colourful and friendly.
Save it as a file called index.html in this folder.

Cline will show you what it plans to do. Read it, then click Approve or Save. A new file appears on the left. Well done — you have made a web page.

Diagram 5 — Talk to it in the panel on the right. Your new file appears on the left.

That is it — you are set up

Everything from here on is just asking for more things. The next two sections show you the two most fun ones: drawing diagrams, and putting your page on the internet.

4. Making a diagram

Asking for a diagram is the same as anything else — describe it in plain words. Try one of these:

Draw a diagram
Draw me a diagram of my family tree: me at the top, my two children below, and their children below that. Make it a Mermaid diagram so I can see the picture.
Explain something with a picture
Draw me a simple diagram of how a bill becomes a law, in six boxes with arrows. Keep the words short enough to fit in the boxes.

A "Mermaid diagram" is just a way of writing a picture in text. Cline can draw it for you and often shows you the picture straight away. If it only shows text, say: "Show me the picture, not the code."

A good habit

If you do not like the result, just say so in ordinary words: "Make the boxes bigger." "Use different colours." "Less text." It will happily try again. You cannot wear it out.

5. Putting your page on the internet

This is the best part. You will get a real web address you can text to your family. There is no coding and no account needed.

  1. Open your file folder (My Projects). Inside it you should see your index.html file.
  2. Open a web browser and go to app.netlify.com/drop.
  3. Drag your index.html file from the folder onto the big dashed box on that page. (If you have several files, drag the whole folder.)
  4. Wait about ten seconds. A web address appears, ending in netlify.app. That is your own page, live on the internet.
  5. Click it, then copy the address from the top of your browser and send it to anyone you like.

Diagram 6 — Drag the file in, get a link out.

Congratulations

You have just built a page on the internet by describing it in English. That is a genuinely useful skill. Most people who have been doing this for years started exactly here.

6. Things to try next

Copy any of these into Cline, changing the bracketed parts. Half the trick is simply asking.

Prefer to be talked through the setup?

If you would rather not follow written steps, copy the prompt below into a free chat assistant you already use — ChatGPT, Grok, or Gemini — and it will walk you through this whole guide one step at a time, answering your questions as you go.

Paste this into ChatGPT, Grok, or Gemini
I am a complete beginner with computers. I want to set up an AI helper on my Windows computer. Please walk me through it one step at a time, in plain English and with no jargon. The plan is: (1) install a free program called Visual Studio Code, (2) add an AI helper called Cline to it, (3) create a DeepSeek API key and put about $5 of credit on it, (4) connect Cline to that key using the model deepseek-flash, and (5) use it to make a diagram and a small web page. Ask me to confirm each step before you move to the next one. If I get an error message, ask me to type it out for you and help me fix it. Never ask me to share my API key with you.

Things to ask your helper

A card for someone
Make me a pretty birthday card as a web page, for my granddaughter who is turning 8. Pink and purple, with big friendly writing. Save it so I can send it to her.
Something useful
Make me a simple shopping list web page where I can type items and tick them off, and it saves what I typed even if I close the page.
Recipe collection

A timeline

The magic sentence

Whenever you are unsure, add: "Explain this to me like I am a beginner, one step at a time." It will slow down and be gentle with you.

7. If something goes wrong

Nothing here is a disaster. Here are the four things that most often go wrong, and the fix.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
It says the key is wrong or not allowed The key was not copied fully, or has a space on the end. Go back to DeepSeek, make a new key, copy it carefully, and paste it again. Copy the whole thing, including the sk- at the start.
It says there is no money or no balance You have not topped up yet, or the credit ran out. Go to DeepSeek, open Billing, and add another small amount.
It says the model cannot be found An old name is selected. Older versions still list names that no longer work. Set the model to deepseek-flash, typed exactly like that.
It changed something you did not want It was in "Act" mode and you did not read the plan. Switch it to Plan mode. In Plan mode it only suggests and never changes anything. Then approve when you are happy.
The universal fallback

If you are lost, type this into Cline and it will walk you back: "I am stuck. Tell me exactly what to click next, in plain English, as if I am a complete beginner."

8. Three rules to remember

1. Keep your key private

Your sk-... key is like a card number. Never share it, screenshot it, or paste it into a chat. If you think it leaked, go to DeepSeek and delete it, then make a new one.

2. Do not paste private things

Anything you give it leaves your computer and is read by the company. Do not paste passwords, bank details, or other people's private information. For normal hobbies, recipes, family projects, and learning, it is fine.

3. Check important facts

It is confident and friendly, and occasionally wrong — especially with dates, numbers, and names. For anything that matters, check it against a source you trust. It is a wonderful assistant, not an authority.

9. You are finished

Here is everything you have set up. If all of these are true, you are done.

  • You have VS Code installed on your computer.
  • The Cline helper is installed and you can open its chat panel.
  • You have a DeepSeek key saved somewhere safe.
  • Cline is set to deepseek-flash and can answer you.
  • You have made a file, and you know how to put it on the internet.
Go and play

The best way to learn now is simply to ask for things you actually want. Start with something small and fun. You genuinely cannot break it.
